رحماني

"From Raʾs al-Ḥadd, in the Dāmānī, steer Maṭlaʿ al-Ḥimārayn, the rising of the two donkeys, south-east. Each dusk, while the horizon still shows, take al-Qutb on the kamal."

"When al-Qutb falls to seven isbaʿ, you stand on the parallel of Calicut. Turn to Maṭlaʿ al-Ṭāʾir, due east, and run down the parallel until the land rises."

"Al-Qutb is not the pole. It walks a small circle around it. It stands true at nightfall, when al-Farqad lifts its arm up and to the left of it. After midnight it rides high and lies."

"The low coral islands lie across the parallel of Calicut, a day and a night west of the coast. They show no fire. By night, listen for breakers. Better: cross them by day."

"Sea snakes come two days before Malabar. Green water, one day. Never close a lee shore in the dark: strike sail, lie to, and wait for dawn."

"One isbaʿ is a finger's width at arm's length. Each one you fall is some thirty farsakh of southing, about a day's sailing on a slant."

Your log speed is a guess. The current is a liar you can't see. Only the kamal tells the truth, and only about north and south.
