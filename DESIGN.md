# Personal observatory — nasser1931.com

**Current direction:** Space-centric, requested 20 September 2026. The next local iteration follows the user-supplied `nasser1931.com — Redesign.html` artboards (21 September), translated into responsive Astro pages with real content.

## Experience

Nasser's personal home for work, racing, notes, and reading. The opening pairs a large name and direct introduction on the left with a full-width three.js solar system behind it, its sun right of centre: the sections of the site as bodies (Earth is Projects, Mars Races, the asteroid Notes, Saturn Reading, the Sun home), each a click-to-open card that links to its page. The static Arabia-facing Earth is the poster, and the fallback without WebGL or with reduced motion. The home-base caption and the texture credit sit bottom-left, and the drag hint and section links bottom-right. On phones, the whole system sits below the text and links, edge to edge. A compact shipped/racing/reading strip leads into a full-width Rihla showcase, followed by three supporting cards. Native links and ordinary document scrolling keep everything directly accessible.

## Visual language

- Outfit for spacious display type and supporting text; self-hosted IBM Plex Mono 400 for labels and log details. Seven shared type tokens define labels (14px), body (16px), lead (20px), card titles (24px), headings (40px), display (64px), and hero (104px), with smaller display steps at responsive breakpoints. Eyebrows are muted, uppercase, 0.08em tracked mono across every page. Literata remains for long-form article prose.
- Default dark: blue-black #080D16, surface #101824, text #EEF2F8, muted #A6B3C6, amber #E8BD85.
- Light alternative: lunar gray #EEF1F4, text #172337, muted #526077, bronze #8C5827. Preserve the saved `np-theme` preference. The hero follows the selected theme. CSS and color-scheme metadata both default to dark; the saved preference updates the class, metadata, native controls, and the toggle action label. The outlined theme button uses the same treatment in both modes.
- Planetary artwork supplies the atmosphere. Use restrained rules around real content. No decorative section, card, or list numbers; preserve meaningful dates, counts, the 1931 brand, and authored research numbering. No simulated telemetry or navigation puzzles. The hero orrery is the one moving element, it is optional (plain links sit under it), and it does not run under reduced motion.
- The homepage hero is full-bleed, about a viewport tall (680-960px), with the copy in the shared 1360px container (32px desktop gutters, 20px mobile) laid over its left. The camera is fitted so Saturn and its ring are never clipped and no body or label sits under the copy. The hero keeps its near-black space background, and its copy the dark palette, in the light theme. Below 900px the hero is the copy followed by a full-bleed square canvas, with no text over it. The header, content, and footer share a grid. Compact standalone links and controls provide at least 44px targets. Internal links use →, back links ←, external destinations ↗. All links have visible focus; reduced motion disables smooth scrolling.

## Artwork

`src/assets/earth-arabia.png` is a 1254 × 1254 transparent illustration generated with built-in imagegen on 21 September 2026. It faces the Arabian Peninsula with Oman, the Horn of Africa, Iran, and India visible. Astro Picture serves AVIF/WebP widths 400, 640, 960, and 1254. Complete sphere, blue atmosphere, warm dawn, no rectangular background or clipped limbs. The artwork and Muscat marker are illustrative, not a precise map or NASA photograph; the marker uses one normalized position on all viewports. It is now the poster and fallback for the orrery, with empty alt text; the Muscat marker hides once the 3D view is live. Earlier Earth assets are unused prior versions.

The orrery uses Solar System Scope planet, ring, and sun textures (CC BY 4.0, based on NASA imagery), credited with a link in the hero caption. Sources are in `src/assets/orrery/`; the mapping, size and speed rules, and lighting notes are in the Orrery section of `CLAUDE.md`. Size follows how much is in a section and speed follows how recently it changed, so a quiet section drifts. Only dates the data contains are used.

`ProjectPreview.astro` renders authentic screenshots from `src/assets/projects/`: two Rihla demo screens (shared-expense ledger and settlement) and Einstein's actual travel desk. `sources.json` records the pinned official-repository source URLs and provenance. Rihla screenshots contain seeded demo data and are captioned accordingly. The homepage lead panel is about 560px tall on desktop, with large staggered phones cropped at its lower edge; mobile restores the full images and natural panel height. All previews use Astro Image, responsive WebP variants, dimensions, descriptive alt text, and lazy loading.

## Content and routes

- Projects: `/builds`; Notes: `/stupidshit`; Races: `/races`; Reading: `/reading`; Sky: `/sky`.
- Preserve existing article URLs, collections, RSS, and sitemap. Life (`/field`) and The Silent Creep (`/paper`) were retired on 27 September 2026; their URLs 301 to `/races` and `/builds`.
- Rihla leads with verified App Store and Google Play links, with the repository as a secondary link. Einstein’s Travel Bureau is featured on the homepage. The welcome post remains at their original URLs but are not promoted on the homepage. The homepage shelf shows real books as spines, beginning with current reading.
- Projects uses two alternating visual showcases: Rihla and Einstein’s Travel Bureau. The remaining tools form a catalogue grouped by purpose. Use specific labels from builds.json and verified destinations, without a redundant year column or blanket Live status. Bite is intentionally MIT open source. All selected projects use the same surface and border treatment in both themes. Rihla and Einstein include real visuals. On the homepage Rihla is the lead feature; the three supporting cards (Race log, Einstein, Worlds I’m in) share a title size and card treatment. Worlds I’m in shows up to four series with any finished or current book, current series first, as thin segment tracks with a finished/total count, and links to the full tracks on Reading.
- The Race log card (September 2026, replacing the retired coach) is non-dev personal content: the next race from `next-race.json` with a browser-recomputed countdown, the latest result with a swim/bike/run split bar, and recent races. `/races` lists every race. Results come from recorded intervals.icu activities in `races.json`; never invent times, placings, or races. `/coach` was removed and 301-redirects to `/races`.
- Reading syncs live from Hardcover (`source: "hardcover"`) with reading progress; a StoryGraph CSV import remains a manual fallback. Label the source accurately either way. Private tags and reviews are not site content. Races and reading use committed data with dates. Avoid invented activities, book reviews, project statuses, or reading totals. No Nedd claim without supporting content.
- Notes uses a spacious journal index, with the actual latest entry featured.
- Reading uses a current-book feature beside the wishlist, totals and import provenance, series tracks with the order I read each series (main books and companions by finish date, same-day reads grouped, gaps between reads, the current book last; undated reads are counted, never placed), charts of recorded finish years and formats, and a compact finished table with year jump links. `reading-series.json` records publisher-verified main-book catalogues and selected companions; the matching helper derives finished, current, and wanted states from the imported shelf. Unmatched books are "not logged", never assumed unread. Companions count separately, and current reads do not count as completed. Preserve partial dates, fractional ratings, and the undated group; do not invent queue priority, covers, or reviews; show reading progress only when the synced source supplies it. Charts describe the imported records rather than a complete lifetime reading history, with gaps made explicit. Series links jump to the corresponding book record; on narrow screens the tracks scroll within their own rows.
- Real published covers appear on the homepage and throughout Reading. `src/data/book-covers.json` records explicit title-and-author matches, verified image dimensions, and source/edition provenance independently of the StoryGraph snapshot, so reimports retain the cover catalog. These are representative English editions, not claimed exact audiobook/print editions. Open Library images use their direct cover URLs per their public-display guidance; author and publisher sources fill verified gaps. `scripts/cache-covers.mjs` (run in CI) mirrors covers to `public/covers/` with a dominant colour in `cover-cache.json`; pages prefer the local copy, then the remote URL, then a styled title block. Covers load lazily, reserve their space, keep all artwork visible, and have a clear missing-image state. The homepage shelf draws spines in each cover's colour, with the current book pulled out; hover or focus pulls out another. Adjacent titles provide the accessible text; decorative cover images use empty alt text. Keep source credits below the shelf.
- Sky shows the owner's own night-sky photos, newest first: a full-width image, then the date, title, location, camera, lens, and a capture line. These are real content, not atmosphere, so they carry descriptive alt text. Exposure values describe one frame; a stacked image says so and gives the frame count only when it was recorded. The homepage closes on the latest photo. Only the "Something here sparked a thought?" line and button sit on the image, over the dark sky; the title, capture line, and "All photos" link sit below it. The block keeps its own dark palette in both themes. Without a photo, the plain closing note returns. A photo can carry a timelapse, shown under its details with native controls and a first-frame poster; it never autoplays, and it stays off the homepage.
- Entry pages use large Outfit headings and a readable Literata prose column. The 404 page uses the same type, colors, and a direct route home.
