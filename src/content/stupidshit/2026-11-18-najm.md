It is the 3rd of September, 1491. You have forty horses for the Zamorin of Calicut, a dhow, the south-west monsoon behind you, and your pilot is dead. What he left you is a kamal: a small board on a knotted string that measures how high a star sits above the horizon.

That is the whole navigation kit. There is no map and no longitude, just the sky.

## How to play

- Steer by the Arab star compass. Its points are named after where bright stars rise and set.
- At night, hold the kamal up to Polaris and read your latitude in *isbaʿ*, finger-widths.
- When the reading says you are at Calicut's latitude, turn east and run down it. That is how it was actually done.
- There is a current you cannot see, and reefs off the Laccadives that you will find the hard way.

## What's real

The stars come from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, precessed back to 1491. That matters more than it sounds: Polaris was 3.5° off the pole then, so it only gives you a true reading at dusk. The music is generated live in the browser.

A bot that turns east at 11.3°N reaches Calicut in about six game days, 31 times out of 32. You will do worse.
