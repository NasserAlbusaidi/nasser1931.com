Up in the jebel, a mother well taps the aquifer, and a tunnel carries the water for kilometres underground to the village. You are the *'arif*, the one who decides whose turn it is.

Water in a falaj is divided by time, not volume. A turn is an *athar*, about half an hour, and the full rotation (the *dawran*) runs for days. Every house owns shares, inherited and sold over centuries, and nobody owns the water itself.

## How to play

- **By day**, time the gate by the shadow of a stick crossing marker stones.
- **By night**, time it by the stars of the lunar mansions cresting the ridge. In manual mode you press Space when you see one cross, and if you're late, somebody notices.
- Rent out and auction the lots the falaj owns, pay diggers to clear silted shafts, or call a *faz'a*, where the whole village turns out to dig.
- Gate-time drift turns into grievances at the majlis.
- Then the drought comes, and somebody offers you a motor pump.

It's the most Omani thing I have made. A bot that keeps up with repairs and follows the drought rules survives five of six seeds if it petitions the wali. If it takes the pump money, it survives four.
